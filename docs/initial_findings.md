# Initial Weighted Findings

## Analysis status

These are descriptive, unadjusted results from the Statistics South Africa General Household Survey 2025. They show associations, not causal effects. A subsequent modelling stage accounts for age, sex, province, geography, household income per person, household size and food insufficiency. See [Adjusted Education Findings](adjusted_findings.md).

## Validation

The analysis includes 22608 sampled children aged 0 to 17, representing approximately 21.06 million children after applying the person survey weight.

|Living arrangement|Calculated|Stats SA published|
|-|-:|-:|
|Both parents|31.37%|31.4%|
|Mother only|45.92%|45.9%|
|Father only|4.20%|4.2%|
|Neither parent|18.52%|18.5%|

The close match confirms that the child filter, parent co-residence logic and person weights reproduce the official benchmark.

## First comparisons

### Education

Among children aged 5 to 17 with a valid attendance response:

|Living arrangement|Currently attending education|Repeated a grade|
|-|-:|-:|
|Both parents|97.52%|4.29%|
|Mother only|96.99%|5.26%|
|Father only|97.38%|5.06%|
|Neither parent|95.47%|6.61%|

Attendance is high across all four groups. Children living with neither parent have the lowest unadjusted attendance rate and the highest unadjusted grade-repetition rate. The differences are associations and may partly reflect age, province, rurality, income, household size and other unequal conditions.

### Household food insufficiency

The measure indicates that food for children was insufficient seldom, sometimes, often or always.

|Living arrangement|Child food insufficiency|
|-|-:|
|Both parents|12.05%|
|Mother only|23.30%|
|Father only|22.19%|
|Neither parent|23.80%|

The largest descriptive gap is between children living with both parents and the other three groups. This does not demonstrate that family structure causes food security. Household income, employment, geography, grants and household composition are likely important explanatory factors.

### Social protection

|Living arrangement|Any social grant|Child Support Grant|Foster Child Grant|
|-|-:|-:|-:|
|Both parents|46.25%|44.34%|0.01%|
|Mother only|79.30%|76.66%|0.04%|
|Father only|64.54%|59.75%|0.10%|
|Neither parent|80.83%|75.15%|2.23%|

Higher grant receipt should be interpreted as social-protection reach and underlying eligibility, not as a negative child outcome. Foster Child Grant receipt remains uncommon even among children living with neither parent and cannot be used as a complete foster-care classification.

## Responsible preliminary conclusion

The first results suggest that parent co-residence is closely intertwined with household resources and social protection. Children living with neither parent show somewhat lower school attendance and higher grade repetition, while food insufficiency is substantially more common in the mother-only, father-only and neither-parent groups than in the both-parent group. These differences must be tested after accounting for socioeconomic and geographic conditions before drawing stronger conclusions.

## Subsequent analysis

Household context profiles and adjusted education point estimates are now complete. Remaining extensions are to calculate design-based confidence intervals, examine sequential specifications, analyse ECD indicators for the applicable young-child population and turn the validated results into an interactive dashboard and concise portfolio presentation.
