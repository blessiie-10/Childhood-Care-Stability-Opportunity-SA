# Project Brief

## Project title

Childhood Care, Stability and Opportunity in South Africa

## Portfolio purpose

Demonstrate practical skills in public-data research, ethical problem framing, data cleaning, survey-weighted analysis, visualisation, documentation and evidence-based communication for data analyst and data science opportunities.

## Core research question

How are children's living arrangements, household resources, caregiver stability and exposure to adversity associated with education and wellbeing outcomes in South Africa?

## Sub-questions

1. What proportion of children live with both parents, their mother only, their father only or neither biological parent?
2. How do school attendance and grade repetition vary across these living arrangements?
3. How do food security, income, household size, geography, services and grant access vary across the groups?
4. Do observed education differences become smaller after accounting for age, sex, province, geography and household resources?
5. Among young children, what do available ECD, caregiver, stimulation and discipline indicators show?

## Unit of analysis

Children aged 0 to 17 living in private households or worker's hostels covered by the GHS 2025.

## Primary comparison

The project compares observable parent co-residence, not family worth or care quality:

* Both parents in the household
* Mother only in the household
* Father only in the household
* Neither parent in the household

## Measurable outcome framework

|Theme|Example indicators|
|-|-|
|Education|Attendance, grade repetition, ECD attendance, school feeding|
|Health|Self-reported health, medical-aid access, disability indicators|
|Stability and resources|Food insecurity, household income, household size, dwelling and services|
|Social protection|Child support grant, top-up, care dependency grant, foster care grant|
|Early care|Main caregiver relationship, learning activities, selected discipline practices|

## Responsible-use commitments

* Do not describe one family arrangement as inherently superior.
* Do not calculate a single probability that a child will be "successful".
* Use clear, measurable outcomes and report uncertainty.
* Separate descriptive comparisons from adjusted statistical models.
* Avoid causal claims from cross-sectional survey data.
* Do not use foster-grant receipt as a complete foster-care classification.
* Do not infer adoption status because the GHS does not directly measure it.
* Interpret adversity and discipline measures using the source's definitions.

## Deliverables

* Clean Python analysis pipeline
* Jupyter notebook explaining each step
* Source and variable trackers
* Analysis-ready child-level file generated locally
* Summary tables and charts
* Power BI dashboard or Python visual report
* Ethical findings summary
* GitHub README with methods, results, screenshots and limitations
* Short presentation for interviews or LinkedIn
