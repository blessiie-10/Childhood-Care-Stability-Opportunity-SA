Why I built this

A personal question, approached with care.

Growing up as a foster child made this project personal to me. I saw children raised in different household circumstances face very different levels of stability, support and opportunity.

I wanted to move beyond assumptions and compare measurable outcomes across children living with both biological parents, a mother only, a father only, or neither biological parent.

◎

Family structure is not destiny.

The focus is on conditions around children—not ranking families.

How the analysis works

Reproducible, weighted and cautious.

The workflow joins person and household records, validates the living-arrangement distribution and compares outcomes before and after adjustment.

  1. 01
    Join

    Link person and household files using the survey key.

  2. 02
    Validate

    Check weighted groups against Stats SA’s 2025 baseline.

  3. 03
    Compare

    Estimate education, support and resource indicators.

  4. 04
    Adjust

    Account for selected demographic and household factors.

What this project can show

Nationally weighted patterns and associations between living arrangements, household context and selected outcomes.

What it cannot show

Causation, direct adoption status or a complete identification of every child in foster care.

The takeaway

“Children’s outcomes are shaped not only by who they live with, but by the stability, resources and opportunities around them.”

The strongest story in the data is not a ranking of families. It is a reminder to examine the conditions that help children thrive.
